# testimonial-launch

Ship first, then let your users do the talking. This is the launch pattern
behind Meta's Muse rollout: the announcement was carried by reposted and
quoted user testimonials, not by ad copy from the company account.

## The pattern

- **Users make the claim, you amplify it.** A founder saying "looks very
  strong" converts better than any landing page headline because the
  credibility belongs to them.
- **Quote-post, don't screenshot.** Quoting keeps attribution live, lets the
  algorithm credit both accounts, and invites the original poster to engage.
- **Minimal commentary.** One line of context, then get out of the way. The
  user's words do the work.

## Soliciting testimonials that convert

Ask for specifics, not praise:

- What exact task did it complete?
- How long did it take vs. doing it manually?
- What did it make or save, in dollars or hours?

"Saved me 3 hours on my expense reports" beats "this is amazing" every time.
Specificity is what makes a testimonial believable and shareable.

## Curation

- Pick users with credibility in your target audience: founders, builders,
  and practitioners whose followers trust their judgment.
- Favor concrete outcomes over enthusiasm.
- Spread across use cases: one testimonial per job-to-be-done shows breadth.
- Never script or edit testimonials into marketing speak. Real voice
  outperforms polished copy; audiences can smell a rewrite.

## Cadence

Drip, don't blast. One amplified testimonial per day over a launch week
compounds; ten in an hour looks desperate and trains the algorithm to
ignore you. Pin the strongest one.

## What not to do

- Don't quote-post criticism as if it were praise.
- Don't amplify a claim you can't back up (see `money-challenge` for
  verification discipline).
- Don't pay for testimonials without disclosure. It will surface, and it
  will cost more than it bought.
