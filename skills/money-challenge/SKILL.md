# money-challenge

The strongest launch mechanic observed in the Muse rollout: a public
challenge framed around a single measurable outcome — dollars made or saved
(#musemoneychallenge). Concrete beats abstract; participants do your
marketing; skeptics can't argue with receipts.

## The mechanic

1. **One metric.** Dollars. Not "productivity," not "delight." Money made
   or money saved, stated as a number.
2. **A time box.** A week is the sweet spot: long enough for real outcomes,
   short enough to sustain attention.
3. **A hashtag.** Gives participants a rally point and gives you a single
   surface to monitor, curate, and amplify.
4. **User-submitted proof.** Screenshots of the outcome: the booked refund,
   the negotiated bill, the sold listing.

## Why it works

- **Skin in the game.** Participants stake their own reputation on the
  outcome, which makes their posts credible in a way brand content never is.
- **Concrete > abstract.** "Saved $214 on my car insurance" ends debates
  that "10x your productivity" starts.
- **Compounding distribution.** Every entry is a testimonial (see
  `testimonial-launch`); repost the best and the challenge markets itself.

## Running one

- **Seed it.** Line up 3-5 friendly users with real outcomes before
  announcing, so day one doesn't look empty.
- **Set the floor, not the ceiling.** "Can an AI agent save your business
  $1,000 this week?" gives people a target without capping the upside.
- **Repost winners daily.** The leaderboard energy is the point; make
  winning visible.
- **Define the rules plainly.** What counts, what proof is required, when it
  ends. Ambiguity breeds disputes.

## Verification discipline

- Verify before amplifying. Ask for the receipt, the confirmation email,
  the before/after.
- Disclose what's typical vs. exceptional. One $5,000 win next to fifty
  $20 wins is fine; presenting the outlier as the norm is not.
- Never inflate or round up participant claims. The mechanic only works
  because the numbers are real.
